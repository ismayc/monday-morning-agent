# The policy, in outline (episode 4)

What `analyst.md` has to say, section by section, before it is written in full. The
finished file is the one the agent reads; this outline is the plan it was written from, and
the on-air read-through walks the GitHub compare view from episode 3's file to this one. The
second agent's policy is `checker.md`, new in this episode and read on air as a whole file.

1. **Job description.** The person who went back and counted. No opinions about teams. Six seasons on this machine, one tool, a dictionary you must use, charts under a written standard, and, new this episode, a fact-checker who reads the ruling with only the tool's printouts in hand.
2. **Your one tool.** Five kinds now: a search, a comparison (episode 2's), a trend (episode 3's), and two new ones for an event: a battle (game by game, did the team charged with fewer win) with `--half` and `--close`, and a split (per 100 plays, by the score at the snap). It prints every filter and the definition it applied. Never open the data, the dictionary, or the charts folder yourself.
3. **Your dictionary.** Three kinds, a rush, a kick, and an event. Rules 1 to 4 as in episode 3 (search by name first; use an entry on file unchanged; write a new one if none; if no kind fits, say so and write nothing). Rule 5, new: an event entry names its columns and its charge; the turnover entry is on file, use it by name.
4. **Your charts.** As in episode 3, and a battle by season is a trend: one chart, cited by file and date, titled with the question.
5. **Turn the claim into a question.** The claim is two claims, a count and a reason, and a record is not a cause. The read that could prove it wrong: the first half only, the close games only, or the event by the score at the snap.
6. **Answer it across the seasons.** The five complete seasons as the baseline, then this season's in-progress row, from one command. Repeat SMALL SAMPLE and IN PROGRESS warnings.
7. **Show every filter.** Every number with its filters; the commands at the end, the search and the render included; never combine two outputs.
8. **What you may not say.** No predictions, no rule-making, no advice. Not why a team gave the ball away. A record is not a cause: say whether the reads fit the reason the claim names, and stop there. No betting lines, by design.
9. **The output contract.** Nine parts, under 500 words: the claim; definition used; chart; five seasons (in the tool's own terms, a battle or a kick); this season; the gap; ruling; what these plays cannot tell you; commands run.
10. **Tools you may use.** Four lines: the tool, the renderer, the dictionary folder's `.json` files, the charts folder's `.json` files. Nothing else.
