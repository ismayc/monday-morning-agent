Ask five fans what the tush push is.

You get five answers. They are different definitions, and the data can see some of their clauses and not others. 260,927 plays, 31 columns each, and not one column says who pushed.

1 The quarterback sneak, with teammates pushing from behind. What it would take: who lined up behind the quarterback, and who pushed. Not in the data: the columns name who carried the ball, not who pushed him.
Not in the data
2 A quarterback run with two or fewer yards to go, from under center. What it would take: who carried (the primary passer, the quarterback as the data can see him), the yards to go, and the shotgun column at 0. Every clause is a column.
The data can see itThe one the agent writes
3 Any play from the Eagles' formation. What it would take: where every player lined up before the snap. Not in the data: the only formation detail in the 31 columns is shotgun or not.
Not in the data
4 Any quarterback run with one yard to go, any formation. What it would take: who carried, and the yards to go. The data can see both, and the explorer can build it.
The data can see it
5 Any rush on third or fourth and short. What it would take: the down, the yards to go, and a rush. The data can see all three, and the explorer can build it. But it is not the play: it counts every handoff too.

Where the argument stands

In May 2025 the NFL voted on a proposal to ban the play. It got 22 votes to 10. A rule change needs 24, so the ban failed. No ban was proposed for 2026, so the play is legal this season.

The agent writes the second definition. It is the closest the 31 columns come to what fans mean, and its last line says what it cannot see: who pushed. That definition is page one of the dictionary. Every later ruling that measures the play has to use it and cite it, and a different definition has to be a new entry with a new name. After the episode airs, the explorer page lets you change the definition yourself and watch the answer move.
