Holds, as a steady edge

Over 2026, the defined play converted 82.0% of 128 plays, every other rush with the same yards to go 67.7% of 477, a gap of 14.3 points. Take away the Eagles and the gap is 14.5 points, so it is not one team's play. The play converts about ten points more often than everything else at that distance, season after season. Whether that is grounds for a ban is a judgment about safety and fairness, and no number on this sheet settles it.

The verdict follows a rule written in season-sheet.mjs, so you can disagree with it. It reads the all-downs gap: under 100 plays matching the definition is too few to rule; a gap of 8 points or more that stays at 5 or more without the Eagles holds, as a steady edge; a gap under 4 does not hold as stated; between the two is mixed. Whether the play should be legal is not on this sheet. This is the page's rule, not the agent's. Every number is the tool's.

Measured with

"The quarterback takes the snap under center with one yard or less to go and runs straight ahead, with teammates pushing him from behind. In these plays it is any run by the team's primary passer, not from shotgun, with one yard or less to go, on any down."

dictionary/tush-push.json, written 2026-10-26 by the analyst, episode 2. Rules: the runner is the team's primary passer that game (the player with its most pass attempts); not from shotgun; any down; 1 or fewer yards to go. Compared with every other rush with the same yards to go. Cannot see: who pushed the runner or whether anyone pushed at all, so it counts every under-center quarterback sneak, pushed or not, and it cannot tell a sneak from a designed quarterback run off tackle.

The name search first: node ./query-plays.mjs --search "tush push" --seasons 2026 found the words in 0 of 18,561 descriptions. The play has no name in the data; the definition above is how the tool finds it.
