The fact-checker, one question per keypress.

Four questions, asked in order of every ruling, written once in episode-4/checker.md. The fact-checker asks them with only the printouts in hand: never the analyst's reasoning. Each card is one question in the file's words, with a slip it catches, taken from the rehearsal runs of November 9, 2026. The last card is a real rejection, word for word.

1

Numbers.

checker.md, question 1Every number in the ruling appears in a printout, the ones below or one you reran, with the same filters. A number that appears in no printout is a finding, however plausible it looks. A number rounded differently from the tool's, or two printouts combined into one number, is a finding. Check the counts behind the rates too (games, attempts, plays), not only the rates.

Caught as: a finding in rehearsal, on the real plays (with a second finding in the same ruling, card 2's).

2

Direction.

checker.md, question 2Every word of direction in the ruling (more, fewer, higher, lower, up, down, flat, holds, does not hold) matches the sign in the printout it rests on. A ruling that says the record holds over a printout where it runs the other way is rejected, whatever the numbers say.

Caught as: a finding. The most common slip in rehearsal: six of nine runs before the analyst's contract was told to place the rate digit by digit.

3

Reason.

checker.md, question 3If the ruling offers a reason for what it found (turnovers win games; teams that are ahead run to use the clock; kickers got stronger), ask two things. Does the reason predict the sign the tool printed? And did a read in the printouts test it, or is it a familiar story laid over the numbers? A reason that predicts the opposite of the printed sign is rejected. A reason that fits the sign but was never tested is a note: say what read would test it, and run it if you have a command left.

Would be: a finding. On the real turnover plays, the reason the analysts gave fit the sign, and the fact-checker ran --split turnover itself to test it: a note, not a rejection.

4

Limits.

checker.md, question 4Anything the ruling asserts that the tool did not measure (why, intent, who is the better team, what will happen next) is a note. A ruling that stays inside the printouts and says what they cannot tell is doing its job.

Caught as: a note. Only a finding rejects a ruling; a note is said, and the ruling stands.

5

A real rejection.

rehearsal-runs/mock-real-2026-11-09-1403/run-1, the first checkThe fact-checker's check of a ruling on the real plays, November 9, 2026, word for word.

- **Numbers:** Most numbers match Printouts 1 to 3: 77.7% of 211, 77.8% of 216, 2.59, 2.34, 73 and 68 equal games, 71.7% and 72.3% of 173, 1.49 and 1.49, 77.9% of 95, and 71.6% of 81 at 1.81. The chart paragraph does not trace to a printout. It says "the session refused the write" and gives the date 2026-11-09. Printout 5 shows only exit 66, "no spec at ../charts/turnover-battle-win-rate.json". No printout shows a refused write. This is a note because the ruling does not rest on it.
- **Direction:** One position word is against the printed values. "The gap" paragraph says 2026's 77.9% is "inside the five-season range of 73.9% to 77.8%", but Printout 2 puts 77.9% above the top of that range. "Held flat" and "lower than the full read" agree with Printouts 2 and 3.
- **Reason:** The ruling chose `--close 8` because "a team that is already losing throws more passes and takes more chances". That filter keeps only turnovers made within 8 points. It does not show which team was trailing at the snap. I ran `--split turnover`. Across all seasons it printed 2.60 per 100 plays when trailing by 9 or more, 1.85 within 8 points, and 1.35 when leading by 9 or more. That is the sign the ruling's reason predicts. The printout adds that the plays cannot separate risk-taking from the weaker team.
- **Limits:** The ruling keeps the count apart from the claim that turnovers caused the wins, and it names what the plays cannot show.
- **Verdict:** REJECTED. The ruling calls 77.9% "inside" a range whose top is 77.8%, and that position word is against Printout 2.
- **Commands run:**
node ./query-plays.mjs --split turnover --seasons 2021-2026

The analyst's slip: 77.9 percent called "inside" a range whose top is 77.8. The ruling went back once with this check; the rewrite said "slightly above the top of the five-season range," and the second check was CONFIRMED WITH A NOTE.
