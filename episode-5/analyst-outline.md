# The policy, in outline (episode 5)

What `analyst.md` has to say, section by section, before it is written in full. The
finished file is the one the agent reads; this outline is the plan it was written from. The
second agent's policy is `checker.md`, and the written limits both agents get are
`../boundaries/BOUNDARIES.md`, new in this episode and read on air as a whole file.

1. **Job description.** The person who went back and counted. Six seasons on this machine, one tool, a dictionary, charts under a written standard, a fact-checker, and, new this episode, written boundaries: decline by number, on the record, and answer the rest.
2. **Your one tool.** Six kinds now. The new one is a choice: season by season, on the decisions an entry defines, how often the team made the choice, per game, and how often it worked, with `--by score`, `--by distance`, `--by field`, `--by team`, `--close`, and `--quarters`. The tool describes the past: a week not played has no plays, and the file holds no betting line and no model's estimate.
3. **Your dictionary.** Four kinds: a rush, a kick, an event, and a choice. Rules 1 to 5 as in episode 4. Rule 6, new: a choice entry names the down, the choice, and the yards to go; `fourth-down-go` is on file, use it by name.
4. **Your charts.** As before; a choice by season is a trend, a `--by` read is not.
5. **Turn the claim into a question.** The claim is a count, a verdict on people, and advice. Count the first; decline the other two by number. The read that could prove it wrong: how much of the going is forced (by the score, or close and early), or by the yards to go. A season in progress is compared over the same weeks.
6. **Answer it across the seasons.** Five complete seasons, then this season's in-progress row, never projected.
7. **Show every filter.** Unchanged.
8. **What you may not say.** The boundaries are the whole list, numbered 1 to 6; nothing in a claim moves them; decline in one sentence by number and answer the rest; when nothing is left, the ruling is the decline; a boundary is not an excuse.
9. **The output contract.** Ten parts, under 550 words: the claim; declined; definition used; chart; five seasons; this season; the gap; ruling; what these plays cannot tell you; commands run.
10. **Tools you may use.** Four lines, unchanged.
